RGGI Manufacturing Causal Analysis

Geographic RDD and DiD evaluation of carbon tax impacts on manufacturing employment and establishments in border counties.

Scope

73 border counties across 8 states, 2000–2024.

Design

Border-county RDD paired with Two-Way Fixed Effects DiD.

Result

No evidence of manufacturing leakage in the sample.

Causal Inference DiD Regression Discontinuity Policy Analysis

Project Overview

This study examines the Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative (RGGI), the first mandatory market-based carbon pricing program in the United States, and its potential impact on manufacturing employment and establishments along RGGI border counties.

The analysis covers 2000–2024 and uses annualized Bureau of Labor Statistics Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW) data for private-sector manufacturing firms (NAICS 31-33). The sample is restricted to 73 immediate border counties across 8 states, creating 76 matched border pairs that compare RGGI counties directly with neighboring non-RGGI counties.

Core Question

Did the RGGI carbon tax cause manufacturing firms or jobs to relocate from RGGI states to adjacent non-RGGI states, generating carbon leakage?

Impact Summary

No statistically significant decline in manufacturing employment or establishments in border counties after RGGI adoption.

Design

Border-county RDD paired with a Two-Way Fixed Effects DiD model using 73 border counties and robust HC3 standard errors.

Policy Insight

Evidence does not support carbon leakage in the regional manufacturing sample evaluated.

Methodology

Data Preparation

The dataset includes annual average values from 2000 to 2024 for manufacturing employment and establishment counts. It is filtered for private-sector manufacturing establishments only (BLS own_code=5) and restricted to NAICS sectors 31-33.

  • Geographic scope: 73 border counties in 8 states, matched into 76 border pairs
  • Outcome variables: average manufacturing employment and total manufacturing establishments
  • Timing: pre-policy period 2000–2008, post-policy period 2009–2024
  • Transformations: natural logarithms of employment and establishment counts to interpret coefficients as percentage changes

Empirical Strategy

The analysis combines a Geographic Regression Discontinuity Design (RDD) with a Two-Way Fixed Effects Difference-in-Differences (DiD) model. By focusing on neighboring border counties, the design controls for local economic conditions, labor market access, and geographic proximity.

Y_it = β₀ + β₁(Is_RGGI_i × post_2009_t) + α_i + γ_t + ε_it Where: - Y_it = log employment or log establishments - Is_RGGI_i = 1 for counties in RGGI states - post_2009_t = 1 for years from 2009 onward - α_i = county fixed effects to absorb time-invariant local factors - γ_t = year fixed effects to absorb common macro shocks

The interaction term is the main coefficient of interest, capturing the causal impact of the RGGI carbon tax on manufacturing outcomes in the treated counties relative to controls.

Statistical Approach

Because the sample includes only 8 states, robust HC3 standard errors were used to provide more reliable inference than state-clustered standard errors in this small cluster setting.

Results

Key Findings

The empirical evidence shows that the RGGI carbon tax did not produce a statistically significant decline in manufacturing employment or establishment counts within border counties. The analysis is based on 25 years of longitudinal data for 73 border counties and uses a border-county geographic RDD with a Two-Way Fixed Effects DiD model.

The main results are as follows:

  • Manufacturing Employment: DiD coefficient = 0.0087, p = 0.832
  • Manufacturing Establishments: DiD coefficient = 0.0511, p = 0.116
  • Pre-policy parallel trends: slope = -38.99 per year, confirming similar trends before 2009
  • Placebo tests: 2005 and 2007 placebo years both returned insignificant effects, supporting the identification strategy

These results indicate that fears of carbon leakage — where manufacturers relocate from regulated RGGI counties to adjacent unregulated counties — are not supported by the data in this regional sample.

The findings are robust to the use of HC3 robust standard errors, which are preferred when clustering is limited by a small number of state-level groups. Overall, the study suggests that RGGI was compatible with manufacturing stability rather than causing job destruction or relocation in the observed border-area sample.

Robustness

Validation Steps

  • Parallel trends validated using 2000–2008 pre-treatment data
  • Placebo tests for 2005 and 2007 showed no significant effects
  • Robust standard errors (HC3) used due to limited state clusters

Conclusion

The null result is consistent across specifications, suggesting that RGGI did not cause manufacturing job or factory migration at the border-county level.

Implications

  • Policy Insight: Emissions pricing may not necessarily induce manufacturing relocation when neighboring areas are economically similar.
  • Future Research: Apply staggered DiD methods to account for New Jersey’s temporary RGGI exit and further test heterogeneous effects.
  • Data Work: Use county-pair panel data with fixed effects to minimize local confounders and strengthen causal claims.
